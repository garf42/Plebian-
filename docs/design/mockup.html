PL-02

How a day fills

Omi → phone → Pi → page
  1. Omi
    Records all day and streams to Plebian on your phone over Bluetooth, paired the way it pairs with Omi’s app.
  2. Omi
    Double-tap to mark the moment. In the phone’s range, the app writes the mark at once and gives the Omi a short buzz; out of range, the Omi gives itself a long one. A single tap does nothing, so a bump can’t leave a mark, and a three-second hold turns the Omi off.
  3. Phone
    Plebian sends the audio on to the Pi as it arrives, over your tailnet, at home or away. Everything Omi’s app would send to Omi’s servers goes to the Pi.
  4. Phone
    Out of the phone’s range, the Omi records to its own 512 MB and syncs when it’s back, stamped with its own clock, as it does with Omi’s app.
  5. Pi
    The Pi transcribes as audio arrives and labels each speaker; a voice it doesn’t know becomes a question in Open, and once you name it in your own words it is known from then on. Each conversation lands as it ends, as underlay rows, and each mark becomes a pointer into one.
  6. Pi
    Mail, texts and the calendar land the same way, fetched by code as they arrive. The agent is called only when what lands, a calendar entry coming due included, lights an open loop or a kept order.
  7. Memory
    The night’s read gets the day with what is open, the kept orders and the marks. The agent writes the entry. A heard answer comes back as a question, and nothing heard closes anything.
  8. Plebian
    The page is up: the entry on top and the marks on the ribbon. Whatever you say back about a heard line settles it.
  • Conversations and messages land on the Pi through the day. The night’s read runs once, at the hour you’ve come to ask for it, over all of them.
  • Plebian’s background service does two jobs: it holds the Omi’s Bluetooth link and the line to the Pi, so audio flows and notifications arrive without any push service.

PL-04

Marks

finding what mattered
  • There are two ways to mark, and both write one row. Double-tap the Omi, or long-press any line in the app. The row holds a time, the stretch of talk around it and an optional note.
  • A double-tap reaches the phone at once, so the mark is on the day’s ribbon right away. A short buzz means it’s saved. A long buzz means the phone was out of range: the audio still syncs, and long-pressing the line later marks it. The two are the Omi’s own shortest and longest buzzes, 100 and 500 ms.
  • The night’s read reports every mark. That line becomes the mark’s title, so search finds a mark by what was said.
  • There are three ways back to a mark: Journal → Marks, newest first; the pick on each day’s ribbon; or search with Marks on.
  • A mark says the moment mattered, not that the words were heard right. Heard words still wait until you’ve said something about them.

  • The sweep covers Duet and other agents, Omi firmware and app releases, security notices and anything that broke. The gear in the header opens the brief; each item says what changes and why, and you approve or skip it.
  • New feeds and tools come in the same way, and every new step that can change something starts out drafted.

  • Each sweep restores last night’s copy of the Pi and replays a page from it, so the backup is proven, not assumed.
  • Replay gates every update. If a rebuilt page differs from the one you lived, the update waits, with the difference shown.
  • Hands-on work goes through your coding agent’s remote session, from the same screen.
PL-07

Where it runs

one box at home
  • Plebian is a phone app set up like Omi’s: it pairs with the Omi, streams its audio, syncs what it recorded out of range and takes its taps. It reuses Omi’s open-source device code, MIT-licensed, for pairing, streaming, taps, offline sync, setting the clock and firmware updates, so Omi’s own fixes reach it through the sweep.
  • Everything Omi’s app would send to Omi’s servers goes to the Pi instead: live audio, offline sync, conversations and voice profiles. The Pi is reached through your tailnet, the same way at home and away. Omi’s app can’t do this itself: pointed at your own transcriber, it still forwards the transcript to Omi’s backend.
  • Mail comes straight to the Pi over IMAP with an app password, and the calendar from its secret iCal address: no Google Cloud project to set up. Replies go out over SMTP exactly as drafted, on your word; calendar changes go through the agent’s browser, drafted the same way.
  • Texts reach the Pi as a linked device of Google Messages, the way Messages for web does, because no app on the phone can read encrypted RCS chats. That pairing is unofficial, so if Google breaks it the feed goes quiet.
  • Every feed is watched for silence: a quiet longer than any it has had becomes a question in Open, so a dead sign-in can’t pass for a slow week.
  • Each night the store, the audio and every message as received are copied to a second disk, and offsite encrypted.
  • The background service that keeps the Omi connected also holds the line to the Pi, so notifications arrive without any push service.
  • Speech is typing: the keyboard’s voice input fills the composer.
  • Nothing on the phone talks to any server but the Pi. If the home line can’t take a connection from outside, as with carrier-grade NAT, the tunnel runs through a relay that sees only encrypted packets.
